# 02 · Visual rules: dos and don'ts

Source: soul.md, "Universal Thumbnail Principles". These are the repeatable behaviours behind high-CTR thumbnails: readable in the feed, a click decided in under a second.

## Universal
- One clear idea and one dominant focal point. Viewers decide in under a second.
- The thumbnail and the title reinforce the same promise.
- High contrast (luminance + colour) is non-negotiable.
- 2-3 elements at most: subject + text + optional supporting object.

## Fonts and text
**Do**
- Bold, condensed, high-impact sans: Anton, Bebas Neue, Impact or similar.
- 1-4 words (house practice: 2-4, split over two lines).
- Big enough to read at mobile size.
- Contrast against the background of about 4.5:1 or more.
- Put the text in negative space or over a high-contrast area.
- ALL CAPS (house style), or mixed case only if it stays instantly readable.

**Don't**
- Thin, script or decorative fonts.
- More than one short phrase.
- Text over busy areas or areas of a similar colour.
- Small text that disappears when the image shrinks.
- Low-contrast pairs (light grey on white, dark blue on black).

## Subject, emotion and composition
**Do**
- A clear face with a readable emotion (surprise, anger, joy, shock, worry) when a person is involved.
- A large subject as the clear focal point.
- A clean cut-out or isolation when it adds clarity.
- Rule of thirds or a strong visual hierarchy.
- A subtle rim light or edge definition so the subject separates from the background.
- Clean, high-contrast facial features: eyes, mouth and expression must read at small size.

**Don't**
- Tiny faces or several faces competing.
- A subject flat against a background of a similar tone.
- Faces over-edited into something uncanny or heavily filtered that loses the emotion.
- Secondary objects that clutter the frame and dilute the subject.
- A neutral or unreadable expression when emotion is the hook.

## Lighting, contrast, highlights
**Do**
- High overall contrast: bright highlights, deep shadows.
- A rim or edge light to separate the subject.
- More local contrast on the face and the key elements.
- The brightest point pulls the eye to the subject or the text.
- **Grayscale test:** the hierarchy must still be obvious in grey.

**Don't**
- Flat, even light with no separation.
- A background that competes in brightness with the subject.
- Shadows so crushed that facial detail is lost.
- Subtle gradients that vanish at small size.

## Colour and palettes
**Do**
- Complementary or warm-on-cool pairs: yellow/black, red/yellow, blue/orange, yellow/purple. House pair: teal/orange.
- A limited palette of 2-3 dominant colours.
- High-contrast text: yellow or white on dark, black or dark on light.
- Saturated colour used for emphasis, not everywhere.
- Colour contrast that still works in grayscale.

**Don't**
- Low-contrast pairs (red on green, blue on purple, similar mid-tones).
- Lots of colours competing.
- Muted or desaturated palettes that melt into the YouTube feed.
- Text in a colour with low contrast against what's behind it.

## Repeatable workflow (6 steps)
1. Define the single idea the thumbnail has to say.
2. Choose and isolate the main subject (a face whenever it's relevant).
3. Apply strong separation: rim light, cut-out or contrast.
4. Add 1-4 words of bold, high-contrast text if needed.
5. Limit the palette to 2-3 high-contrast colours.
6. Confirm it matches the title's promise without repeating it.
