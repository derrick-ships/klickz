# 11 · Derrick's rules (16 Nov 2026)

Everything Derrick knows about thumbnails, in his own words. These are his edits, so they beat every other file (10, priority 1). They apply to every thumbnail, whatever the style. Where an older file or the house look says otherwise, this file wins, except where the user asked for something specific in the request.

1. The eye scans a thumbnail in about 0.02 seconds. It moves in a Z from top left to bottom right, so put the hook top left and the proof bottom right.
2. Use three elements at most, like a face, some text and one object. Three is the most the brain can read instantly. At most, not always three: fewer is fine.
3. Make one element clearly the main focus. Everything else supports it, so the eye knows where to land.
4. Add a twist the title doesn't say. A thumbnail that only repeats the title gives no reason to click. Thumbnail and title must complement each other.
5. Avoid red, white and black as the main colours. YouTube's own page uses them, so the thumbnail blends in. (They can still be small accents, such as text outline or a small mark.)
6. Put the most intense colour on the twist. The twist is what makes people curious, so it should catch the eye first.
7. Show the result the viewer wants as a picture. The eye reads the thumbnail before the title, so the picture has to carry the promise. (Educational niches: how-to, tutorials, learning, fitness, money, skills.)
8. Light the face from one side. Look for a small triangle of shadow beside the nose (Rembrandt light). Phones default to flat light, and flat light looks amateur.
9. Make the face show the feeling the title promises. The subject's expression tells the viewer how the video will feel.
