# 05 · Legal and accuracy

A thumbnail is the most aggressive claim in the package and the one that gets screenshotted. Same rules as the script, applied more strictly.

## Text about a named person
- **ESTAFA, MINTIERON, FALSO, ROBO, CORRUPTO, CRIMINAL** and similar words on a named person: only if the script backs it with something proven or clearly attributed.
- If it doesn't, use one of three exits:
  1. **Turn it into a question:** "¿MINTIÓ?", "¿ARCHIVADO DOS VECES?", "¿QUIÉN NO PAGÓ?". The "¿?" also gets the red accent.
  2. **Attribute it:** "DICE LA FISCALÍA", or a real quote from the script in quotation marks.
  3. **Point at the fact, not the person:** "NO CUADRA", "ESTO NO ES NORMAL", "LO ESCONDIERON".
- **Presumption of innocence:** if there's no conviction, the image doesn't suggest one. No handcuffs, prison bars, prison clothes or mugshot framing on someone who hasn't been convicted. A file folder, a gavel or a calendar are fine as context.
- Quotes: only a real phrase that appears in the script. Never put words in someone's mouth.
- No explicit swearing. "JODIDOS" is the ceiling.

## Accuracy
- Never promise what the recorded video doesn't deliver. If the thumbnail says "¿QUIÉN NO PAGÓ?", the video has to answer it.
- Keep names, numbers and dates exactly as the script says them.
- Don't borrow authority ("EXPERTOS", "CIENTÍFICOS") unless the script cites them.
- Superlatives and urgency ("LO PEOR", "ESCÁNDALO") only if the script itself frames it that way.

## Face and image
- Always use a real photo of the person, faithful to the reference. Don't caricature, deform or change their features.
- The expression can be picked (evasive, angry, proud), but it must exist in a real photo. Don't make the model fake tears or a grimace.
- Party symbols and flags (M-19, the war-to-the-death flag) only when the script talks about them, as with the Abelardo beret video.
