# 01 · Principles: how to think before you prompt

## Start from the script, never from a blank page
- Find the **grievance**: the fact or contradiction the viewer (Colombian critics of petrismo) already feels in their gut.
- Find the **payoff** the video actually delivers. The title names *what*; the video delivers *why*; the thumbnail adds the *tension* in between.
- **Anchor + bet:** an anchor the viewer recognises in under a second (a known politician's face, an event, a place) plus ONE bet that creates tension. Two or three ingredients at most.
- Before anything else, state the video in 10 words or fewer. If you can't, the concept isn't clear yet. Refocus before you write a prompt.

## Title and thumbnail are a pair, not twins
- They must **reinforce the same promise** and never contradict each other.
- The thumbnail **never restates the title.** The title gives context; the thumbnail creates tension and opens a new question.
  - Canonical example: title "Why Nokia Lost the Smartphone War" + a Nokia next to an iPhone with "THEY SAW IT COMING", not "NOKIA FAILED".
  - House examples: title about Petro attacking Char → "¿Y QUÉ HIZO ÉL?"; title about Hollman Morris's case being archived → "¿ARCHIVADO DOS VECES?"; title about petrismo attacking Abelardo's beret → "¿Y SUS BANDERAS?".
- The thumbnail carries the same **entity + conflict** as the title, through the face, the object or the text.

## One emotional lever per package
Name it before you write:
- **MIEDO** (fear): what's being lost, what's coming, what threatens the viewer. Text such as ESTO ES GRAVE, SE VIENE EL FINAL, ¿ESTAMOS JODIDOS?
- **INTRIGA** (intrigue): something hidden, contradictory or unexplained. NO CUADRA, LO ESCONDIERON, ÉL SABÍA, ¿QUIÉN NO PAGÓ?
- **DESEO** (desire): a win, the rival's defeat, justice, being proven right. OBRAS, NO ODIO, JUGADA MAESTRA, HUNDIDO.
The face's expression must match the lever: evasive or guilty for intrigue, tense or worried for fear, confident or proud for desire.

## Cover test
- The thumbnail alone, with no title, must make sense and make someone want to click.
- The title alone, with no thumbnail, must make sense too.
- If the thumbnail only works once you've read the title, it's a caption, not a thumbnail.

## Write for the right viewer
Petrismo critics in Colombia are the audience. Let the wrong viewer scroll past. Don't soften the package to please everyone, and don't overreach beyond what the script says (see 05).

## Three thumbnails, three promises
Each video gets three prompts: **Recomendado** (paired with the main title) plus one for each of the two alternate titles. They must be three *different* angles, not three versions of the same text. That's what makes Test & Compare useful.

## Fit the channel, not the theory
The YouTube course, 1of10 and NexLev are direction, not law. They sell outlier tools and contradict each other on text length, numbers and so on (see 10). The channel's own results come first: when Derrick edits a page, his edits beat every rule here.
